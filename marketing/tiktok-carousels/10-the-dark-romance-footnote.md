# The dark romance footnote

A romance reader develops an inconvenient interest in the setting.

Tone: Suggestive, absurd. Eight slides. Fictional adult POV, written for a brand-owned account.

Audience: Adult romance readers who fall into research rabbit holes.

## Alternate opening hooks

- Came for the morally grey man. Stayed because the castle made no sense.
- The fictional duke was hot. His inheritance arrangements were suspicious.

Use the first hook in the script below. Test the second by replacing slide 1 only.

## Slide script

Only the quoted copy goes on the slide. Visual notes are production direction.

### Slide 1

> Came for the morally grey man. Stayed because the castle made no sense.

Visual: An original fictional romance cover beside a castle sketch.

### Slide 2

> He had seventeen titles, no visible job, and a suspicious amount of candle money.

Visual: Candlelit desk with an absurd handwritten household bill.

### Slide 3

> At first I accepted this. I was busy.

Visual: Bookmark abruptly tucked into the novel. Let implication carry the joke.

### Slide 4

> Then he inherited another estate. I needed an organisational chart.

Visual: A messy fictional family tree.

### Slide 5

> One search became six tabs about titles, land, and inheritance.

Visual: Tabs rendered as generic research labels, no historical claims.

### Slide 6

> Now I am pausing the romance to question the household budget.

Visual: Novel beside a notebook labelled "who pays the staff?".

### Slide 7

> I made a Learnfold course on the history behind the setting. The duke is under review.

Visual: Actual historical course plan. Use a specified place and period in the example.

### Slide 8

> Still would. But I have questions about the estate.

Visual: Return to the candle and the fictional family tree.

## Why the swipe earns its place

The app appears as the home for a funny, specific rabbit hole. The flirtation survives the learning.

## Caption

Fictional adult-reader POV. The romance was interrupted by administrative concerns. What tiny fictional detail sent you researching?

Use an original fictional book and adult characters. No copied passages, explicit scenes, or claims about a real novel that has not been read.
