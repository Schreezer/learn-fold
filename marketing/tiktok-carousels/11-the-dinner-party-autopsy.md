# The dinner party autopsy

Social embarrassment becomes a reason to investigate one thing properly.

Tone: Socially anxious, biting. Eight slides. Fictional adult POV, written for a brand-owned account.

Audience: Adults who have bluffed their way through an intimidating conversation.

## Alternate opening hooks

- I said "exactly" seven times at dinner. I understood nothing.
- Everyone at dinner had an opinion. Mine was "exactly."

Use the first hook in the script below. Test the second by replacing slide 1 only.

## Slide script

Only the quoted copy goes on the slide. Visual notes are production direction.

### Slide 1

> I said "exactly" seven times at dinner. I understood nothing.

Visual: Elegant dinner table, tiny tally marks on a napkin.

### Slide 2

> Someone mentioned a film director. Everyone nodded. I nodded with commitment.

Visual: Anonymous dinner silhouettes. No real director needed yet.

### Slide 3

> Then someone asked which film was my favourite.

Visual: Large question over a suddenly empty background.

### Slide 4

> I said, "I love the earlier work." I had invented an entire viewing history.

Visual: Wine glass halfway to mouth, freeze-frame feeling.

### Slide 5

> On the way home, I finally looked him up. The films looked interesting. Annoyingly.

Visual: Late-night train window, cinema programme.

### Slide 6

> I wanted a way in that did not begin with pretending I already belonged.

Visual: Notebook question: "What should I notice while watching?"

### Slide 7

> So I started a Learnfold course on reading films. Then actually watched one.

Visual: Real film-language course plan. The movie plays elsewhere, not inside a fake app feature.

### Slide 8

> Next dinner, I said "I have only seen one." The conversation was better.

Visual: Same table, no tally marks.

## Why the swipe earns its place

The ending rewards honest beginner status. It avoids selling intelligence as a prerequisite for being accepted.
