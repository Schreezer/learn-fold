# I wanted to be her

A crush, envy, and a museum visit become difficult to separate.

Tone: Queer-coded, intimate, playful. Eight slides. Fictional adult POV, written for a brand-owned account.

Audience: Adults who have confused admiration, attraction, and wanting someone's life.

## Alternate opening hooks

- I could not tell if I wanted to date her or become her.
- She explained one painting. I developed a personality problem.

Use the first hook in the script below. Test the second by replacing slide 1 only.

## Slide script

Only the quoted copy goes on the slide. Visual notes are production direction.

### Slide 1

> I could not tell if I wanted to date her or become her.

Visual: Two adult silhouettes in a gallery.

### Slide 2

> She stood in front of a painting for ten minutes. I stood next to her pretending to see it.

Visual: Back view in front of original or licensed abstract art.

### Slide 3

> Then she pointed out a tiny detail. The whole painting changed.

Visual: A visible detail in the chosen artwork. Research it before naming any historical interpretation.

### Slide 4

> I wanted to be able to do that. Also, her number.

Visual: Gallery ticket with a handwritten number obscured.

### Slide 5

> I got the number. Then spent the train home reading about painting.

Visual: Ticket and train window. No actual contact details.

### Slide 6

> By midnight I had forgotten to draft a charming text. I had questions about colour.

Visual: Notebook with two paint swatches and a blinking message cursor.

### Slide 7

> I started an art course in Learnfold. Our next museum trip will have follow-up questions.

Visual: Real art-course plan, framed by the ticket.

### Slide 8

> Update. It was both.

Visual: Two gallery tickets. Sparse type, warm finish.

## Why the swipe earns its place

The opening ambiguity resolves on the last slide. The course grows out of an actual moment of curiosity.

## Caption

Fictional POV. Art appreciation got complicated. What interest arrived disguised as a crush?
