# He liked me better confused

A condescending romance loses its appeal once the narrator starts asking better questions.

Tone: Dark dating humor. Eight slides. Fictional adult POV, written for a brand-owned account.

Audience: Adults who have mistaken confidence for expertise.

## Alternate opening hooks

- He liked me better when I said "wow."
- He said I would not understand. I took that personally.

Use the first hook in the script below. Test the second by replacing slide 1 only.

## Slide script

Only the quoted copy goes on the slide. Visual notes are production direction.

### Slide 1

> He liked me better when I said "wow."

Visual: A fictional chat bubble reading "it is complicated".

### Slide 2

> Every dinner came with a lecture about his startup. I was the audience.

Visual: Two plates; a napkin full of arrows.

### Slide 3

> I asked what one word meant. He said, "It is hard to explain."

Visual: The word "moat" written alone on the napkin.

### Slide 4

> So I started a beginner business course. Partly curious. Mostly annoyed.

Visual: Notebook titled "words he will not explain".

### Slide 5

> Next dinner, I asked, "Who actually pays for it?"

Visual: The same table. One plain question.

### Slide 6

> He said, "You have been watching too many videos." He still did not answer.

Visual: The reply in small type; unanswered question stays large.

### Slide 7

> My Learnfold course had a lesson to return to. His pitch had another adjective.

Visual: Actual relevant lesson or course-plan screenshot. No fake financial performance.

### Slide 8

> I ordered dessert. Asked again.

Visual: Dessert fork beside the unanswered napkin.

## Why the swipe earns its place

The question is concrete and the consequence is comic. Learnfold supplies a starting point, not a claim of instant expertise.

## Caption

Fictional POV. "It is complicated" has had a very comfortable career. What word did someone use to avoid explaining themselves?
