# Wait, How? by Learnfold

Main Learnfold content-account direction, approved in conversation on 2026-09-07. Curiosity itself is the reason to explore. Saved for Someday remains an optional campaign about postponed interests.

## Core positioning

> Something catches your attention. Learnfold helps you understand it, then follow the questions it creates.

Account promise: "Understand the things you keep wondering about."

People can learn because it is enjoyable. They do not need to feel unproductive, embarrassed or dissatisfied first. Curiosity Quench emphasizes reducing scrolling and taking action; that is its positioning, not a requirement for Learnfold's audience. [Curiosity Quench](https://www.curiosityquench.com/), reviewed 2026-09-07.

## Audience

People who enjoy understanding how things work and following a question beyond its first answer. Age and occupation do not define the account's editorial audience.

Examples include students exploring something outside their studies, working professionals following an interest in the evening or on a weekend, and older learners investigating something they have always wondered about. These are possible use contexts, not validated customer segments. Having spare time alone says less than a person's interest in exploring further.

Keep individual posts specific even while the audience and subjects remain broad. A student and a retired person can both wonder how noise-cancelling headphones work.

The main uncertainty is conversion. Enjoying an interesting post does not automatically mean wanting a course. Look for people who finish an explanation and immediately ask another question.

## Product connection

Lead with "Explore how it works." Show the course as a way to continue exploring, ask follow-up questions and return later. For a casual learner, leading with "Generate a course" may sound like homework; that is a messaging hypothesis to test.

Learnfold's current product description supports starting from a topic, link, document or photo, reviewing a proposed course plan, generating lessons progressively and asking questions in the course context. [iOS product description](../apps/ios/fastlane/metadata/en-US/description.txt)

A post should answer its opening question before inviting further exploration. Do not withhold the explanation to force an install. A useful next question creates a natural reason to continue in Learnfold.

## Initial topic mix

These are possible hooks. Research the explanations before production and revise any premise that proves misleading.

| Topic | Opening question |
| --- | --- |
| Everyday technology | How do your headphones cancel a sound before you hear it? |
| Infrastructure | Where does a photo actually go when you put it in the cloud? |
| Space | How can we see something that might no longer exist? |
| Music | Why does the same note sound different on a piano and a guitar? |
| Architecture | How does a skyscraper deal with the wind? |

Start with a small mix of everyday science, technology and space. Music and architecture provide adjacent subjects to test as the account develops.

## Carousel structure

Let the explanation determine the length. Ten slides is no longer a hard creative ceiling. Use these eight story beats as a flexible outline, expanding a beat into separate frames when the mechanism needs room:

1. Ask one concrete, intriguing question.
2. Show the familiar situation that makes the question interesting.
3. Establish the first concept needed to understand it.
4. Show the mechanism with a clear visual.
5. Resolve the opening question in plain language.
6. Add a useful consequence, example or limitation.
7. Introduce a natural follow-up question. Where appropriate, show how to explore it in Learnfold.
8. Close with a memorable observation or invite the audience's next question.

Adapt the slide count to the explanation. Every post should be worthwhile even if the viewer never opens Learnfold.

## Slide lengths and generation budget

Updated on 2026-09-07 after discussing longer carousels. These ranges are editorial starting points to test, not proven performance rules.

| Format | Slides | Good use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quick curiosity | 6–8 | One question and one clear explanation |
| Visual explainer | 10–14 | A mechanism that needs several steps |
| Illustrated deep dive | 15–20 | A journey with discoveries throughout |

Start the first Wait, How? curiosity carousel at about 12 slides, with little text on each frame. A longer illustrated journey is welcome when each frame earns its place. The original eight-slide romance scripts remain unchanged.

For example, "Where does your photo go when you put it in the cloud?" could follow the photo as a character through a phone, network, data centre, storage and retrieval. Each swipe advances the journey. Research and verify the technical explanation before turning this premise into a finished script.

Use additional generation capacity to:

- Create three alternate covers for the same story.
- Replace confusing or inconsistent illustrations.
- Split crowded explanations into readable frames.
- Produce an eight-slide and a fourteen-slide version for a length experiment.
- Develop recurring characters and consistent visual conventions.

Keep the topic, main explanation and visual style comparable in a length experiment. Treat results across separate posts as directional because audience exposure and posting conditions can differ. Check completion alongside saves, shares, follow-up questions and app interest.

Editing question: "Does this slide give the reader a discovery, an explanation, or a reason to want the next one?" Keep it when it contributes; cut it when it only delays the answer.

Choose length from the story and explanation. Subscription price does not establish a useful slide count or guarantee unlimited generation capacity.

## Visual direction

Use approachable cartoon or manhwa illustration with readable captions, recurring visual conventions and clear cause-and-effect sequences. An everyday object or curious character can introduce the question. Let the explanatory image carry the mechanism.

Keep factual diagrams accurate. Use controlled diagram tools when geometry, labels or scientific relationships matter; reserve generated illustration for scenes and expressive storytelling. Verify factual claims and labels before publishing.

This account can remain broadly welcoming. The adult romantic framing of the BookTok and dating accounts is specific to those separate experiments.

## Community research

Study accessible science explainers, engineering demonstrations, everyday technology explanations, astronomy educators, music explainers and architecture creators. Search phrases such as "how noise cancelling works", "everyday science", "how things work", "engineering explained" and "astronomy for beginners".

Notice which questions attract follow-up questions, which visual explanations make a concept click, and where commenters remain confused. Follow and engage selectively. Do not treat browsing as a guaranteed distribution strategy.

## Account priorities and evaluation

Make this the main Learnfold content account. Keep Plot & Footnotes and Better Questions Club as narrower experiments if production capacity permits. Saved for Someday is an optional campaign within the broader curiosity direction; it does not require another account.

Test a small batch across the initial topic mix. Compare completion where available, saves, shares, substantive follow-up questions, profile visits and actual app interest. Track topic and hook so a successful individual post does not get mistaken for proof that every subject will work.

Separate curiosity about the post from interest in using Learnfold. This direction fits the product closely, but audience demand and conversion still need evidence from real posts and users.
