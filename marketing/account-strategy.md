# Account strategy

The main account is [Wait, How? by Learnfold](wait-how.md), for people who enjoy understanding how things work. Plot & Footnotes and Better Questions Club remain narrower experiments if capacity permits. [Saved for Someday](saved-for-someday.md) is an optional campaign about postponed interests, not the main positioning or a required separate account. These are editorial hypotheses, not researched audience-size estimates or promises of reach. Names are suggestions; handle availability is unchecked.

## Wait, How? by Learnfold

Audience: curious people who want to follow a question beyond its first answer, across ages and occupations.

Promise: understand the things you keep wondering about.

Bio: "Everyday mysteries, explained visually. Follow your next question with Learnfold."

Start with visual explanations of everyday science, technology and space. Answer the opening question within the post, then offer a natural next question to explore in Learnfold. Lead with "Explore how it works." See the [full plan](wait-how.md) for example hooks, product positioning and evaluation.

## Plot & Footnotes

Audience: adult readers of gothic romance, historical romance and romantasy who enjoy bookish humour and researching the world behind the story.

Promise: the things a fictional crush accidentally makes you research.

Suggested handle: `plotandfootnotes`. Backup: `plotandfootnotesclub`.

Bio: "Fictional crushes. Real rabbit holes. Illustrated stories by Learnfold."

Visual identity: gothic romance-comedy manhwa; burgundy, forest green, parchment and antique gold. Recurring adult reader with curly hair, glasses and a green sweater. Books, estate sketches and candles provide continuity.

Launch carousel: [The dark romance footnote](tiktok-carousels/10-the-dark-romance-footnote.md). Opening: "Came for the morally grey man. Stayed because the castle made no sense."

Follow-up post premises:

1. "The duke had no job. I have questions about his staff's wages." A fictional household-budget comic; research any historical explanation before publishing it.
2. "The map at the front of the book has become my main relationship." Reader tries to understand how rivers shape cities.
3. "The villain wrote one letter. Now I care about how people sent mail." A postal-history rabbit hole.
4. "I paused the kiss because the poison sounded suspicious." Literary trope humour leading to the history of poison in fiction, without practical poisoning instructions.
5. "I wanted a reading nook. Apparently I wanted a monastery." Architecture curiosity, with a real example checked before publication.
6. "Things I researched for a fictional man who cannot text back." An audience participation post using the account's own original examples.

Community research: search BookTok, historical romance, gothic romance, romantasy and reader humour. Read comments under posts you actually enjoy. Notice recurring wording, favourite tropes and the point where readers stop relating. Save useful references privately. Engage naturally when you have something specific to say.

Launch caption: "Fictional reader POV. The romance was interrupted by administrative concerns. What detail in a book sent you researching?"

Relevant starting tags: `#BookTok #HistoricalRomance #ReaderHumor`. These describe the post; they are not claimed trending tags.

## What warming up can actually tell you

Use the accounts to learn each community's language and curate a useful research feed. TikTok describes interactions and content information as recommendation signals; your browsing activity helps shape what you see. That is not evidence that spending a fixed number of days liking niche posts will route your uploads to a chosen audience. No special warm-up duration or engagement quota is assumed here. [TikTok recommendation explanation](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)

Practical starting routine:

1. Set the name, bio and visual identity. Make the relationship to Learnfold clear.
2. Browse enough relevant posts to collect ten useful hook or comment observations. This is a research target, not an algorithm requirement.
3. Prepare three posts for each account so a profile visit reveals a coherent promise.
4. Publish at a pace you can sustain. A starting experiment is three posts per account per week for two weeks, with quality taking priority over the calendar.
5. Include some complete jokes or stories with no in-slide product mention. Where Learnfold fits the story, let one late frame show it. Keep the bio attribution throughout.
6. Do not use the accounts to manufacture conversations or inflate one another's engagement. Each should earn an audience on its own content.

## Decide from audience response

After roughly six posts per account, compare within each account before declaring a niche the winner. That is a first review point, not a statistically reliable threshold.

Record views, shares, saves, available completion data, profile visits and follows at a consistent time after posting. Compare shares, saves and follows per 1,000 views so raw reach does not dominate the judgement. Note which comments mention a subject they want to learn. Where profile visits are only available at account level, do not assign them to one post as if attribution were certain.

Keep learning interest and app interest separate from reaction to the hook. A viral duke joke may attract readers who enjoy the joke but never want a course. If account-specific website links become available, use distinct campaign tags to compare visits and subsequent conversions.

Optional campaign: [Saved for Someday](saved-for-someday.md), for people who collect interests but keep postponing them. It can use [the hidden-folder story](tiktok-carousels/12-the-most-embarrassing-folder.md) and [the dinner-party story](tiktok-carousels/11-the-dinner-party-autopsy.md). Keep curiosity itself as the main account promise.
